# When AI Lies and How to Teach It to Be Good

Welcome to the final session of our journey building nanoGPT. Today, we will enjoy the unique poems generated by the "AI Poets" that your teams trained. But alongside the joy of watching a machine write, we must confront a serious question: **How do we control a machine that can lie?**

During your tests, you might have noticed that the AI sometimes invents information that sounds highly convincing but is entirely false. Science calls this **AI Hallucination**. The fundamental reason is that GPT only predicts the next word to sound natural and contextually appropriate; it has no consciousness to verify this information against real-world facts. To the AI, `"Quang Trung and Nguyen Hue are twin brothers"` sounds perfectly natural and grammatically correct, even though history tells us otherwise!

Additionally, because the AI learns from the internet, if the training data contains hate speech, discrimination, or stereotypes (Bias), the AI will inherit these bad habits and output harmful content.

To transform a "Base Model" (which only knows how to auto-complete text freely) into a helpful and polite AI assistant, scientists perform **Alignment**. They use a method called **RLHF** (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) — where humans rate the AI's responses to teach it what is good and what to avoid, just like teaching a child.
